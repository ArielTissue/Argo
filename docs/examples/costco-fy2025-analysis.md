# Costco (NASDAQ: COST): FY2025 earnings example

**Historical research example, prepared 2026-10-30 using FY2025 disclosures. Not a current recommendation or a live agent evaluation.** Financial reporting cutoff: fiscal year ended August 31, 2025; release dated September 25, 2025. Accessed October 30, 2026. Later results deliberately excluded to keep this example fixed. Portfolio, price and forward valuation assumptions below are fictional; they describe no author's account.

## Business first

Costco sells merchandise through a membership warehouse model. The research question is whether sales and membership economics translate into durable cash generation, rather than whether the business is simply popular. Business description and model orientation: [FY2025 annual report, Item 1](https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf).

## Analysis: what the framework adds

[Cash-flow quality](../../examples/knowledge/free-cash-flow.md): investigate working-capital timing and maintenance versus expansion spending before extrapolating the increase. Cash flow is stronger in this comparison, but one year cannot establish a long-run trajectory. Keep reported FCF and the SBC sensitivity separate; if valuation models dilution explicitly, check for double counting.

[Moat](../../examples/knowledge/moat.md): a scale-and-member-value advantage is a hypothesis to test with renewal, pricing, supplier economics and returns on new locations. Thin retail margins do not automatically mean a weak business. Conversely, rising fees do not alone prove stronger loyalty: price changes and membership volumes need separation. No ROIC claim is made without reconciling invested capital and tax assumptions.

## Valuation: illustrative sensitivity, not an appraisal

Assume a **fictional USD800 price**, not a historical or current market quotation. Use a ten-year equity cash-flow illustration with year-one cash flow C1, annual growth g, discount r and terminal growth t:

`V = Σ(C1 × (1+g)^(n−1) / (1+r)^n), n=1..10, + C1 × (1+g)^9 × (1+t) / ((r−t) × (1+r)^10)`

| Scenario | C1 USD/share | g | r | t | V USD/share | Margin at assumed 800 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 16 | 5% | 10% | 2% | 241.05 | -231.9% |
| Higher growth | 16 | 8% | 9% | 3% | 372.80 | -114.6% |

The rounded C1 of 16 is an assumption anchored only loosely to the historical adjusted figure. Growth, discount and terminal inputs are chosen teaching sensitivities, not source-backed forecasts. No excess-cash adjustment is added; debt flows and future reinvestment are not separately forecast. A production valuation needs those reconciliations, multiple years, peer context and a justified cash-flow model. These scenarios do not establish fair value, a target price or expected return.

## Bear case and next evidence

[Bear case](../../examples/knowledge/bear-case.md): if retaining member value requires more spending while competitive pricing limits margin recovery, cash per share may trail the optimistic path. Check renewal cohorts, fee-versus-volume contribution, wage and sourcing costs, capital returns and future dilution. Define an observation date and identify the disclosure that could contradict the thesis. These are analyst hypotheses, not claims that deterioration has occurred.
