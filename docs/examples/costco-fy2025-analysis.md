# Costco (NASDAQ: COST): FY2025 earnings example

**Historical research example, prepared 2026-10-30 using FY2025 disclosures. Not a current recommendation or a live agent evaluation.** Financial reporting cutoff: fiscal year ended August 31, 2025; release dated September 25, 2025. Accessed October 30, 2026. Later results deliberately excluded to keep this example fixed. Portfolio, price and forward valuation assumptions below are fictional; they describe no author's account.

## Business first

Costco sells merchandise through a membership warehouse model. The research question is whether sales and membership economics translate into durable cash generation, rather than whether the business is simply popular. Business description and model orientation: [FY2025 annual report, Item 1](https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf).

## Review judgment

The reported year supports a growing, operationally resilient business. It does not
yet establish that repeatable per-share owner cash accelerated at the headline rate:
working-capital effects materially change that reading. This review therefore keeps
member-cohort retention, cash normalization and returns on expansion open. Under the
explicitly fictional price and portfolio assumptions below, the stance is **wait**;
that is separate from judging the company's underlying quality.

## What changed, and what actually drove it?

### Sales: demand, expansion and price are different explanations

Net sales grew by 20,287 million. The annual report attributes 14,788 million to
comparable warehouses; the balance is new-location sales. Comparable growth included
roughly 5% more visits and 1% higher ticket. This is evidence of activity rather than
just price inflation, but it is not a complete acquisition-versus-retention bridge.
The gasoline/FX-adjusted company comparable-sales growth was 7.6%, versus reported
5.9%; those adjustments remove specific effects, not every change in mix.
[Annual report, Item 7, Net Sales](https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf).

**Interpretation:** the year supports a healthy demand-and-expansion explanation.
It does not establish that each new warehouse earns an attractive incremental
return. The next test is comparable growth alongside returns and maturation of new
locations; adding stores can raise total sales even if marginal economics weaken.

Q4 diluted EPS rose from 5.29 to 5.87 and net sales from 78,185 to 84,432 million.
These are year-over-year comparisons of 16-week quarters, not sequential growth.
There is no timestamped consensus estimate in this example, so neither “beat” nor
“miss” is claimed. A share-price reaction would answer a separate expectations
question and is not supplied here. [Release, Q4 statements](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx).

### Membership: a useful moat signal, not a free-standing profit segment

The fee increase contributed approximately 40% of FY2025 membership-fee growth.
Renewal was 92.3% in the U.S./Canada and 89.8% worldwide. The company attributes
renewal pressure partly to online-acquired cohorts; its rate is a lagged measure,
not a real-time response to the latest fee decision. [Annual report, Item 7,
Membership Fees and operating model](https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf).

**Interpretation:** rising fee revenue cannot be read entirely as stronger member
loyalty, because pricing and sign-ups contribute differently. Nor does an aggregate
renewal decline alone prove a broken moat if customer mix changed. Follow comparable
cohorts after renewal and distinguish a mix effect from deterioration within a cohort.

Fees equal roughly 51.3% of reported operating income, but that is a scale comparison,
**not a segment-profit allocation**. The statements do not allocate all membership
servicing and selling costs into a separate fee-profit measure. It would be wrong
to label 51.3% the share of profit mechanically “created by memberships.”

### Operating dashboard: distinguish member count from cardholders

| KPI | FY2024 | FY2025 | Reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Paid memberships, million | 76.2 | 81.0 | +6.3%; not total household cardholders |
| Executive memberships, million | 35.4 | 38.7 | About 47.8% of paid memberships in FY2025 |
| Executive share of worldwide net sales | — | 73.6% | Sales mix, not membership-count mix |
| E-commerce comparable growth | — | 15.6% | 16.1% excluding gasoline/FX effects |
| Net warehouse additions | — | 24 | Expansion contribution distinct from comparable growth |

Membership counts can include recently expired memberships; they are not a perfect
measure of currently paying active customers. Member counts and mix: [annual report,
Item 1, Membership](https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf).
E-commerce and warehouses: [FY2025 earnings release](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx)
and annual report Item 7. A dash means this comparison is not supplied here, not zero.

**Interpretation:** Executive members' disproportionate sales contribution makes
retention and value delivered to high-engagement members a particularly useful
monitoring question. It does not demonstrate that upgrading membership causes a
customer to spend more: heavier shoppers may choose to upgrade. E-commerce growth
also needs mix and profitability context before it is called margin-accretive.

### Profit: reconstruct the bridge rather than calling every margin gain pricing power

The annual operating-income increase reconciles as follows, in USD millions:

`+2,759 merchandise gross profit +495 membership fees −2,156 SG&A = +1,098 operating income.`

Gross profit here means net merchandise sales less merchandise costs. It excludes
fees; total revenue in the headline margin includes fees. These denominators must
not be silently interchanged. The bridge is calculated from the [release's income
statement](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx).

**Interpretation:** merchandise economics and fees together covered higher operating
costs. That supports operating resilience; it does not prove all of the improvement
comes from stronger pricing. A durable low-price business may reinvest efficiency
in member value rather than maximize near-term retail margins. Test whether member
activity and cash returns remain healthy while costs rise, rather than treating a
thin margin as either automatic weakness or automatic virtue.

## Cash quality: why a 20% increase still needs interrogation

The headline SBC-adjusted figure grew 20.07%, but the operating-cash increase was
1,996 million, of which the larger working-capital contribution accounts for 1,153
million, or about 57.8%. Subtract that year's disclosed working-capital contribution
from each year's adjusted FCF as a diagnostic:

| Diagnostic, USD million | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Operating cash flow | 11,339 | 13,335 |
| Less working-capital contribution | 611 | 1,764 |
| Less property/equipment additions | 4,710 | 5,498 |
| Less SBC | 818 | 860 |
| Result | 5,200 | 5,213 |

This bridge is calculated from the [cash-flow statement](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx).
The diagnostic increases only 0.25%. It is **not** a company metric or a claim that
5,213 is the uniquely correct recurring cash flow. Working capital is integral to a
retailer; part of a favorable movement can reflect healthy growth or inventory
management and can recur. Removing it entirely is a stress test, not a forecast.

**What changes in the thesis:** the reported improvement is genuine, but the evidence
for a comparable acceleration in repeatable owner cash is weaker. Do not put the
headline 20% growth straight into a long-run valuation. Reconcile inventory, supplier
terms, accrued obligations, membership deferrals and growth before deciding which
parts can persist. Higher capex may create future earning capacity, but reported
spending does not identify maintenance capital precisely.

SBC remains visible as an economic cost sensitivity. The adjustment is not standardized;
future awards, share counts and repurchases need reconciliation. Applying both a full
compensation subtraction and the same modeled dilution without explanation can
penalize the same cost twice. This distinction follows the
[SBC cash-flow note](../../examples/knowledge/sbc-adjusted-cash-flow.md).

## Moat, balance sheet and capital allocation: keep the verdict conditional

The proposed advantage is a reinforcing loop: a compelling member offer supports
volume, which can support purchasing and operating efficiency, which can help
preserve that offer. The observed sales/renewal evidence is consistent with that
hypothesis, not proof that competitors cannot weaken it. A claim about a durable
moat needs multiple years, comparable customer cohorts and incremental capital
returns. [Framework: moat](../../examples/knowledge/moat.md).

Do not let a cash balance or a high accounting return substitute for a full funding
review. This example does not reconcile debt maturities, lease obligations,
restricted cash or minimum operating cash. Similarly, cash repurchases do not prove
that awards caused no dilution. For capital allocation, the research questions are
whether expansion earns its cost, whether repurchases are priced sensibly and
whether distributions leave operational resilience intact. Until the underlying
notes and multi-year evidence are reviewed, management quality and liquidity are
**unresolved**, not automatically “excellent” because this year's sales improved.

## Valuation: illustrative sensitivity, not an appraisal

Assume a **fictional USD800 price**, not a historical or current market quotation. Use a ten-year equity cash-flow illustration with year-one cash flow C1, annual growth g, discount r and terminal growth t:

`V = Σ(C1 × (1+g)^(n−1) / (1+r)^n), n=1..10, + C1 × (1+g)^9 × (1+t) / ((r−t) × (1+r)^10)`

| Scenario | C1 USD/share | g | r | t | V USD/share | Margin at assumed 800 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 16 | 5% | 10% | 2% | 241.05 | -231.9% |
| Higher growth | 16 | 8% | 9% | 3% | 372.80 | -114.6% |

The rounded C1 of 16 is an assumption anchored only loosely to the historical adjusted figure. Growth, discount and terminal inputs are chosen teaching sensitivities, not source-backed forecasts. No excess-cash adjustment is added; debt flows and future reinvestment are not separately forecast. A production valuation needs those reconciliations, multiple years, peer context and a justified cash-flow model. These scenarios do not establish fair value, a target price or expected return.

### Cash normalization changes the scenario, too

The previous two rows hold year-one cash at 16 per share. A third stress test uses
12 per share, closer to the working-capital-excluded diagnostic of about 11.72 per
share, with 5% growth, 10% discount and 2% terminal growth. It produces USD180.79
(USD89.28 explicit-period value plus USD91.51 terminal value). This does not establish
that excluding working capital is correct; it shows that the starting cash assumption
matters as well as the discount rate.

At 16 per share the fictional USD800 price equals 50 times assumed first-year cash.
To defend it, the model would need materially different growth, duration, cash or
risk assumptions. This report does not solve that gap by calling the business a
high-quality compounder. A hypothetical 20% margin would put review levels at 80%
of the chosen scenario value, not create a personalized buy instruction. Current
market multiples, peers and a verified quote remain outside this historical example.

## Bear case: causal paths and observable checks

| Hypothesis to test | How value could be impaired | Next evidence | What would weaken the concern? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fee growth masks weaker retention | Pricing offsets loss of recurring customers until later renewals | Comparable-cohort renewal and fee/volume bridge | Stable same-cohort renewal after fees reset |
| Favorable cash timing is extrapolated | Working capital reverses while expansion still needs cash | Quarterly cash bridge, inventory and supplier/accrual notes | Cash growth sustained with explainable working capital |
| Expansion raises scale but lowers returns | New assets absorb capital without proportionate cash | Location maturation and consistently defined incremental ROIC | Improving new-location economics across cohorts |
| A premium price needs an optimistic path | Business improves but shareholder returns disappoint | Rebuilt cash assumptions and a verified dated quote | Conservative assumptions support price with room for error |

These are forward-looking analyst hypotheses, not assertions that the problems have
occurred. Distinguish an unresolved observation from an actual thesis-breaking event.
A single lower renewal reading is a request to investigate; it is not an automatic
sell order. [Framework: bear case](../../examples/knowledge/bear-case.md).

## Which signals are supported, and which remain open?

| Proposed signal | Status in this review | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Demand and expansion support sales | Supported for the reported year | Comparable activity and new warehouses both contribute |
| Operating profitability improved | Supported arithmetically | Merchandise gross profit and fees exceed the SG&A increase |
| Recurring cash grows as fast as the headline adjustment | Unproven | A substantial working-capital contribution affects the cash bridge |
| Fee growth proves stronger loyalty | Unproven | Fee changes, sign-ups, cohort mix and recognition timing differ |
| New locations earn durable excess returns | Unresolved | Cohort capital and return reconciliation not completed |
| Stock is attractive to this user at this price | Not established | Valuation is illustrative; the fictional holding already exceeds its cap |

These are conclusions about this evidence set, not closures of invented earlier
predictions. “Supported” means a limited observation is supported; it does not certify
a permanent moat or future return.

## Next-review ledger

This is the first public example, so there is no genuine prior-run prediction to
pretend to close. Start a dated ledger instead:

| Open question | Evidence required next time | Review outcome to record |
|---|---|---|
| Are fee and member effects separable? | Fee recognition, sign-ups and comparable-cohort renewals | Resolved, partly resolved or still unknown, with source |
| How repeatable is the cash increase? | Updated working-capital and capex bridge | Distinguish economics from timing; do not just compare headlines |
| Does expansion create value? | Cohort productivity and capital-return definitions | Revise growth/reinvestment assumptions or leave them uncertain |
| What supports the valuation? | Several years of per-share cash, financing and reinvestment | Rebuild scenarios rather than carry this illustration forward |

Carry unresolved items into the next research task. Do not mark a question answered
merely because a later report repeats a reassuring narrative.
