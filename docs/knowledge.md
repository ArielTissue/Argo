# Default investment knowledge

Argo ships 20 curated notes: 15 concepts and five research workflows, plus an entry
index and a compatibility page. Start at
[investment-framework](../examples/knowledge/investment-framework.md).
`argo init` copies the whole pack to the user's private `knowledge/` directory;
`argo prepare` includes those notes and fingerprints them with the task inputs.
Existing workspaces are never overwritten or automatically migrated. Users can
review and copy chosen notes into an existing workspace, then prepare a new run.

The notes are newly authored English paraphrases of selected distilled concepts,
not exports of an Obsidian vault or translations of book chapters. They contain no
personal holdings, trade records, private allocation rules or exercises. Generic
analysis guidance is separate from `policy.json`; only confirmed user policy sets
portfolio limits. The fictional portfolio and its thresholds remain demo inputs.

## Source orientation

- Pat Dorsey, *The Five Rules for Successful Stock Investing*: competitive advantage,
  cash generation, capital returns and screening. Chapter 3 supports the moat
  orientation, chapter 6 the bear-case orientation, and chapter 12 the quick screen.
- Howard Marks, *The Most Important Thing*: price versus value, second-level thinking,
  permanent loss, uncertainty and the distinction between process and outcome.
- Philip Fisher, *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits*: research depth, concentration
  and reassessing business quality.
- Peter Lynch, *One Up on Wall Street*: business classification, understanding the
  story and adapting research to company type.

Each note identifies its source orientation and original concept name. These are
reading pointers derived from the selected distilled notes, not claims of verified
page-level citations to a particular edition. No book passages are reproduced.
Editorial workflows and modern accounting cautions are labeled as synthesis or
extensions, not attributed verbatim to the authors. SBC-adjusted cash flow is a
nonstandard sensitivity; avoid double counting dilution in valuation.

## Use and limits

The host reads the index, selects relevant concepts, and connects them to dated
company evidence and portfolio context. A wiki link identifies a method; it is not
proof that the cited source supports a company-specific claim. Screening guidance
is not executable policy. Narrative buy/sell workflows do not add CLI verdicts,
order execution, trade simulation or automated semantic validation.

Automated source ingestion, retrieval ranking and knowledge maintenance are future
work. The current pack fits in task context and is loaded in full. It has not been
validated as a profitable strategy, an expert consensus, or the author's personal
mastery of every concept. Users should review, adapt and version their own notes.
