# Quick quality screen

Use a fast screen to decide where to spend research time, not to issue a buy conclusion. Check disclosure availability, recurring operating earnings, cash generation, leverage, earnings stability and unexplained accounting items. Read footnotes before accepting a clean headline. Historical numeric thresholds need industry and cycle context. Small companies, new listings and turnarounds require a deliberately chosen research route; rejecting them automatically would silently combine incompatible screening philosophies. Record the route, exclusions and missing evidence.

Related: [[stock-selection]] · [[return-on-equity]] · [[free-cash-flow]]

## Seven-question worksheet

| Question | What to collect | What to do with a concern |
|---|---|---|
| Is disclosure sufficient for this research route? | Filings, audit status, listing history and ownership | Stop or use a deliberately different route if evidence is inadequate |
| Are operating profits recurring? | Multi-year operating results and segment changes | Separate a funded development phase from an unsupported turnaround story |
| Does the business generate operating cash? | Cash-flow statements and working-capital bridge | Explain persistent financing dependence rather than assuming growth will fix it |
| Are returns supported by reasonable leverage? | ROE history, debt, equity and capital returns | Decompose the ratio before treating high ROE as business quality |
| Are earnings coherent through time? | Several years of margins and major adjustments | Distinguish normal cyclicality from recurring adverse surprises |
| Is the balance sheet understandable? | Debt schedule, guarantees, leases and footnotes | Investigate obligations whose economic substance differs from the headline |
| What else could change the conclusion? | Annual/quarterly filings, governance disclosures, calls and rough valuation | Record unresolved issues instead of treating the screen as completed research |

## Historical numeric prompts

The chapter-12 distilled notes record ROE reference points of 10% for nonfinancial firms, 12% for financial firms and 15% for highly leveraged firms, with persistence over four of five years. These describe the source's historical screen, not validated contemporary cutoffs. They differ from the moat chapter's profitability orientation.

The notes also flag nonbank assets/equity above 4 or debt/equity above 1 as reasons to ask: Is the business stable? Is the debt burden increasing? Do the footnotes reveal obligations not understood? State whether “debt” means interest-bearing debt or total liabilities before applying a ratio; do not compare inconsistent data feeds. Negative equity makes simple ratios especially misleading.

## Route and output

Dorsey's cautious route and Lynch-inspired small-company discovery have different starting populations. Do not silently apply an exclusion from one route to every candidate in the other. Record the chosen route, each question's evidence, pass/concern/unknown status, and the next required document. A completed screen permits further research; it does not justify a purchase.
