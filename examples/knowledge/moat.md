# Economic moat

Start with evidence of sustained economic returns, then explain the mechanism that protects them from competition. Test switching costs, cost advantages, customer networks, brands and legal barriers against retention, pricing and capital returns. Popular products and fast growth alone are insufficient. Separate the size of excess returns from their likely duration. Patent expiry, substitution and customer bargaining power can shorten that duration. Compare several years and suitable industry peers; historical screening ratios are research prompts rather than timeless cutoffs.

Related: [[return-on-invested-capital]] · [[free-cash-flow]] · [[margin-of-safety]]

## Apply the framework

1. Assemble several years of margins, cash generation and capital returns, using a consistent definition and an industry peer set. Look for persistence across difficult periods, not just a favorable year.
2. Identify the mechanism responsible for the returns. Write a competing explanation, such as a temporary shortage, an accounting denominator effect or a cyclical peak.
3. Test how long the mechanism can survive. Identify who could erode it and the observable sign that erosion has begun.
4. Translate durability into valuation assumptions. Do not extend peak margins indefinitely simply because the historical chart looks strong.

| Candidate mechanism | Evidence to seek | Disconfirming observation |
|---|---|---|
| Product differentiation | Customer willingness to pay for a measurable advantage | Competitors match performance and pricing converges |
| Brand differentiation | Pricing or retention after marketing costs | Recognition rises without improved economics |
| Cost advantage | Comparable unit costs and the reason they differ | Scale or process savings are readily replicated |
| Switching costs | Retention, implementation burdens and contract renewals | Customers migrate easily when prices increase |
| Barriers to entry | Network participation, enforceable rights or scarce access | Multi-homing, expiry, substitution or rule changes weaken protection |

## Historical screening prompts, not policy

The distilled chapter notes record FCF/revenue of at least 5%, net margin of at least 15%, sustained ROE above 15%, and ROA around 6–7% as historical orientation. These are different measures, not four interchangeable pass/fail gates. ROIC relative to capital cost offers a further check. Software and intangible-heavy businesses can look unusually strong on accounting ratios; capital-intensive and cyclical businesses need different comparisons. Leverage, repurchases and omitted intangible investment can distort the result.

## Record the result

For each proposed moat, record metric, period, source, mechanism, estimated duration, competing explanation and next falsification signal. If profitability is strong but the cause is unclear, say so. A moat assessment is distinct from the price decision and cannot override portfolio limits.
