# SBC-adjusted cash flow

Show reported FCF and an additional sensitivity of reported FCF minus stock-based compensation (SBC). This is an analytical convention, not a standardized accounting measure or a definition attributed to Dorsey. SBC has an economic cost despite being noncash in the current period. Reconcile dilution, repurchases and share counts. If dilution is already modeled explicitly in per-share valuation, avoid subtracting the same cost again without explanation. Do not compare the adjusted figure across capital-intensive businesses, software firms and property structures without reconciling their economics.

Related: [[free-cash-flow]] · [[cash-flow-quality]] · [[value-traps]]

## Build a transparent adjustment bridge

1. Copy the issuer's FCF definition and amount with the reporting period. Reconcile it to operating cash flow and capital expenditure before adjusting anything.
2. Locate SBC in the cash-flow reconciliation and relevant compensation notes. Confirm that it covers the same period and currency.
3. Show three separate lines: reported FCF, SBC expense, and FCF minus SBC. Label the third line “SBC-adjusted sensitivity”; do not replace the reported number silently.
4. Review diluted shares, awards outstanding and repurchases. A company spending cash to offset dilution has different economics from one allowing share count to rise, but neither makes compensation cost disappear.
5. Explain how the valuation treats share-based awards. If future share dilution is already captured in projected per-share cash flows, subtracting the same economic cost again may double count it.

## Illustrative arithmetic

For a fictional company with reported FCF of 100 units and SBC of 25 units in the same period, the sensitivity is 75 units. This says nothing by itself about fair value: taxes, reinvestment needs, debt and the valuation model still matter. Negative adjusted cash flow is a research concern to explain, not an automatic sell signal.

## Business-model calibration

| Business type | Additional questions |
|---|---|
| Capital-intensive producer | Are depreciation, utilization and replacement spending consistent with future capital needs? |
| Software or platform firm | How do retention, SBC, dilution and repurchases change per-share economics? |
| Property/income structure | Are FFO-style measures, maintenance spending and distributions being reconciled rather than compared directly with software FCF? |

## Limits and provenance

The private distilled note calls this a “true FCF” convention. The public version deliberately uses a narrower label because this is an editorial analytical extension, not a standardized measure and not Dorsey's original definition. It cannot capture every guarantee, financing vehicle or off-balance-sheet obligation. Record remaining blind spots and examine the relevant notes rather than claiming the adjustment has solved cash-flow quality.
