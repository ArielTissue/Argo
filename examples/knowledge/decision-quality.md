# Luck and decision quality

Review information available at the time, stated assumptions, counterarguments, constraints and execution discipline before looking at the eventual return. A good outcome can reward a poor process, and a reasonable process can suffer bad luck. Preserve dated decisions so hindsight cannot rewrite the thesis. Separate changes justified by new evidence from rules invented to excuse an existing position. A retrospective warning is not evidence that a trade was prevented; a price touching a target is not proof that waiting was correct.
